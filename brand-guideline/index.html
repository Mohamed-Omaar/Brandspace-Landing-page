BrandSpace design systemVisual Manual · 2026

A calmer system for a smarter brand.

This manual defines how BrandSpace should look, feel and behave across product UI, dashboards, brand intelligence, marketing surfaces and visual communication. The goal is not decoration. The goal is clarity, calm structure and a recognizable product language.

BrandSpaceYour brand presence, one space.
Core visual idea

Intelligence should feel organized, not complicated.

02 · Principles

How the interface should feel.

Every BrandSpace screen should communicate confidence without visual noise. Hierarchy comes from composition, not from drawing boxes around everything.

01

Calm hierarchy

Separate information through surface, spacing and type. A quiet canvas makes important actions easier to see.

02

Intelligent emphasis

Purple leads interaction. Yellow marks moments of attention. Accent color should guide, not flood the screen.

03

Useful density

BrandSpace is a working product. Keep enough information visible to make decisions, while preserving generous breathing room.

1Surface

Use soft fills to group related information.

2Space

Create separation through consistent gaps and padding.

3Type

Make hierarchy visible through scale and weight.

4Shape

Rounded geometry gives components product character.

5Shadow

Lift only the surfaces that truly need depth.

6Accent

Use purple and yellow selectively for meaning.

03 · Logo system

One mark. Plenty of space.

The BrandSpace mark is strongest when it is simple, high contrast and surrounded by clear space. Do not treat the logo as an illustration.

05 · Typography

Soft confidence, not visual shouting.

BrandSpace uses Inter for Latin interfaces and Cairo for Arabic. Weight should stay restrained: 400 for reading, 500 for emphasis, 600 for headings and controls, 700 only when a strong metric or exceptional emphasis needs it.

Rule 01

One page H1.

A page should have one clear title. Subsections use H2/H3 instead of competing headline sizes.

Rule 02

Do not over-bold.

Use weight to communicate structure. If everything is 700, nothing feels important.

Rule 03

Keep reading widths calm.

Long explanatory copy should usually stay around 55–75 characters per line.

07 · Shape & elevation

Round enough to feel modern. Soft enough to stay serious.

BrandSpace avoids tiny radii and harsh outlines. Controls are compact and rounded; cards are softer and more spacious.

Do not outline every card.

A card should read through its fill, spacing and light elevation. Borders are decorative hairlines only when structure needs them.

Do not stack heavy shadows.

Depth should be felt, not seen as a dark halo. Use the strongest shadow only for overlays.

Keep geometry consistent.

If a control is 44px high, a 12px radius is the normal BrandSpace proportion.

12 · Data visualization

Charts should explain, not decorate.

Use neutral series by default and reserve purple for the most important comparison, selected series or current period. Yellow should annotate, not become the dominant chart color.

One visual question.

A chart should answer one clear question. If it needs a paragraph to explain the axes, simplify it.

Neutral first.

Most series use neutral or lavender tones. Purple marks the selected or key signal.

Yellow = annotation.

Numbers stay readable.

14 · Responsive behavior

Responsive means recomposed, not squeezed.

Do not simply shrink desktop. Change navigation mode, column count, information density and interaction surfaces as the viewport changes.

390Mobile baseline · drawer navigation · single column
768Tablet transition · 2-column selective layouts
1280Desktop working width · persistent sidebar
1440+Wide desktop · larger gutters, not oversized text

Navigation

Desktop sidebar becomes a modal drawer on mobile. Never keep a permanent icon rail on a narrow screen.

Cards

4 → 2 → 1 columns. Keep card padding stable rather than compressing text to fit.

Header

Allow controls to wrap. Hide secondary role/context copy before removing primary actions.

Charts

Simplify legends and reduce visible categories. Horizontal scrolling is a last resort.

15 · Motion

Fast enough to feel responsive. Soft enough to feel premium.

Motion clarifies state change and spatial relationships. It should never delay task completion or become the main visual event.

120 ms · microHover, icon feedback, pressed states.
180 ms · standardTabs, card state, light expansion.
240 ms · panelDrawer, side sheet, larger surface movement.
320 ms · expressive maxRare branded hero transition only.

Prefer ease-out.

Use natural deceleration. Avoid bouncing or elastic behavior in operational UI.

Animate opacity + transform.

Keep motion performant. Avoid animating expensive layout properties.

Respect reduced motion.

All non-essential motion must disappear under reduced-motion preferences.

16 · Visual voice

Premium, intelligent, useful.

The brand should feel like a capable marketing team living inside one product—not like a generic AI tool and not like a traditional enterprise admin console.

Do

  • Use generous white space and soft supporting surfaces.
  • Use purple to guide interaction and selected states.
  • Use yellow sparingly for attention and intelligence moments.
  • Keep copy direct, short and useful.
  • Make AI suggestions explainable and reviewable.
  • Use calm data visualization with one clear highlighted signal.

Don’t

  • Do not outline every component with visible grey strokes.
  • Do not fill whole screens with purple or yellow.
  • Do not use giant marketing typography inside operational UI.
  • Do not make every surface glassmorphic.
  • Do not silently apply AI changes to canonical brand knowledge.
  • Do not overload cards with multiple equal-priority CTAs.
17 · Accessibility & handoff

Accessibility is part of the token.

Contrast, focus, responsive behavior, keyboard access and RTL are design requirements—not QA polish added at the end.

Focus

Always visible.

Use a purple focus ring with a white halo so keyboard focus survives white, tinted and colored surfaces.

RTL

Use logical layout.

Spacing, alignment and directional components must mirror structurally—not through a separate improvised design.

Contrast

Semantic colors are paired.

Error, warning, success and info states use measured text/background combinations and never color alone.

Final handoff checklist

Before a screen is approved.

Uses Inter for Latin and Cairo for Arabic UI.
Uses the spacing scale instead of arbitrary gaps.
Primary controls use purple; yellow remains an accent.
Cards use fill, space and shape before visible borders.
Only one primary action dominates each task area.
Focus, hover, pressed, disabled and error states are defined.
Mobile is recomposed rather than compressed.
RTL works with logical spacing and mirrored direction.
Charts have a clear visual question and readable labels.
AI-generated insight remains visibly reviewable before applying changes.
No text relies on yellow or low-contrast tints for readability.
The result feels calm, useful and unmistakably BrandSpace.
